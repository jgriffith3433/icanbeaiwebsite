# Simulation checklist

The day is one continuous path for one person. Each action starts where the previous one ended. The clock does not jump, and the person never does two physical actions at once.

The clock below is the spine: where they have to be. Micro-behaviors are not extra rows on that clock. They are generated from the pools and rules further down, and they spend time inside the spine.

## Generation rules

1. **Interruptions.** An activity can be broken by a notification, a noise, a bodily need, a forgotten object, or another person.
2. **Failed intentions.** They can start something and not finish it. Open the fridge, take nothing, close it.
3. **Repeated actions.** Recheck a lock, press a button again, reread a message, adjust the same object more than once.
4. **Idle intervals.** A gap is a pause, a fidget, or indecision. It does not automatically become useful work.
5. **Imperfect memory.** They forget what they were doing, misplace an object, and remember a task at a bad moment.
6. **Variable duration.** Sample a duration from a realistic range. Do not assign every action a fixed number of seconds.
7. **Contextual probability.** At a desk, posture and the phone are likely. Vacuuming is not.
8. **State.** Hunger, fatigue, stress, temperature, urgency, and boredom change which actions fire.
9. **Consequences.** An empty glass stays empty until it is filled. A locked door stays locked until it is unlocked. Objects stay where they were put.
10. **Continuous time.** Every action, interruption, and transition consumes time. No overlapping physical actions.

## Corrections to the spine

**Phone.** Do not count "six phone checks." The spine has these intentional phone moments: 7:42 in bed, 10:40 work call, 11:30 text, 12:32 lunch, 3:15 text, 4:05 work call, 7:35 on the couch, 9:25 personal call, 10:35 in bed. Notifications, unlocks, and abandoned opens come from the phone pool and are extra.

**Bathroom.** A visit is entering the bathroom for the toilet. Washing hands, brushing teeth, or a shower in that same entrance is part of the visit, not another one. Passing a mirror in the hall is not a visit.

Awake visits on this day:

1. 7:47 morning, then shower and teeth before leaving the room
2. 10:06 office
3. 12:42 lunch
4. 2:20 office, on the way to water
5. 5:20 office
6. 9:55 before bed, with brushing teeth and changing clothes
7. 10:52 out of bed and back again

The 10:52 trip is a real exit from bed. They are in bed for television, then out, then in bed again. An overnight visit around 2:50 is possible and is not required every night.

**2:20 is one walk, not two.** Desk, bathroom, break room, desk. They do not return to the chair and then leave again for water.

**Sleep is not one block.** Falling asleep takes a variable amount of time. Through the night, sample position changes, brief waking, temperature, and noises. Do not play every possible night event every night.

### 11:00–7:30 Sleep

Falling asleep is its own span and varies. After that, the night is mostly still. Sample a few of these, not all of them:

- Change position.
- Wake briefly and settle again.
- Too warm, push the blanket down. Too cold, pull it up.
- Hear a noise and listen for a few seconds.
- An itch, or a tug on the blanket.
- One bathroom trip, then back to bed. Often around 2:50. Skip it on some nights.

**7:30** The alarm. The next morning begins.

## Behavior pools

Draw from the pool that matches where they are and what state they are in. A drawn action replaces idle time or interrupts the current action. It does not get its own permanent clock entry.

### Mindless actions and micro-breaks

- Stand up, stretch arms overhead, and sit back down.
- Roll shoulders, crack knuckles, stretch neck.
- Shift position in the chair.
- Cross and uncross legs.
- Adjust posture, scoot the chair closer to the desk.
- Rub eyes or scratch an itch.
- Yawn, sigh, take a deep breath.
- Stare into space for 10–30 seconds.
- Stop working and stare at the monitor without processing anything.
- Read the same sentence twice.
- Reach for water, realize the glass is empty, and put it back.
- Check the time without a specific reason.
- Pick up the phone, unlock it, then lock it without doing anything.
- Open an app automatically, scroll briefly, and close it.
- Walk toward another room, forget why, and return.
- Open the fridge, look inside, take nothing, and close it.
- Open a cupboard, inspect the contents, and close it.
- Walk to the kitchen for water, get distracted, and return without it.
- Look out the window.
- Listen to a noise and pause to identify it.
- Hum, tap fingers, bounce a leg, or drum on the desk.
- Fidget with a pen, clothing, or phone case.
- Reposition a pillow or adjust a blanket.
- Scratch the head or rub the face.
- Check whether the door is locked even though it was just locked.

### Bathroom and personal hygiene

- Feel the urge to use the bathroom but postpone it.
- Realize the toilet paper is low.
- Adjust clothing before and after using the toilet.
- Flush, check the result, and flush again if necessary.
- Notice a stain, hair, or something in the mirror.
- Wash hands longer or shorter than intended.
- Dry hands on a towel or shake off excess water.
- Check the mirror briefly while passing.
- Fix hair, eyebrows, or facial hair.
- Apply lip balm or moisturizer.
- Blow the nose or sneeze.
- Cough or clear the throat.
- Apply deodorant again if needed.
- Notice a dirty towel or empty soap dispenser.
- Replace toilet paper, soap, or a hand towel when necessary.

### Kitchen, beyond the meal

- Open the fridge, inspect food, and leave empty-handed.
- Open the fridge twice because the first visit produced no decision.
- Check expiration dates or sniff leftovers.
- Drink directly from a bottle or refill a glass.
- Take one bite of food while preparing something else.
- Taste food while cooking.
- Drop a utensil and pick it up.
- Search a drawer for a utensil that is not where it was expected.
- Open multiple cabinets looking for an ingredient.
- Spill a little food or liquid and wipe it up.
- Wait for water to boil or food to heat.
- Check food before the timer finishes.
- Touch something hot and pull away.
- Change the stove setting.
- Forget an ingredient and retrieve it.
- Eat a small bite while standing at the counter.
- Leave a cabinet or drawer open, then close it later.
- Refill a water bottle.
- Take out trash because the bin is full.
- Notice dishes in the sink and leave them for later.
- Return to the kitchen after sitting down because something was forgotten.

### Phone and computer distractions

- Check a notification without replying.
- Unlock the phone and forget the original intention.
- Open a notification, read it, and return to the previous app.
- Swipe away a notification.
- Check the battery percentage.
- Connect or disconnect the charger.
- Turn the screen on to check the time.
- Adjust volume or brightness.
- Switch between two apps without accomplishing anything.
- Begin typing a message, erase it, and rewrite it.
- Read a message and decide to answer later.
- Check for a reply to a message already sent.
- Open the camera accidentally, or while trying to reach another feature.
- Search for something, get distracted by another result, and abandon the search.
- Notice a low-battery warning.
- Dismiss an update reminder.
- Wait for a page, app, or computer to load.
- Lose connectivity and retry.
- Mistype a password and enter it again.
- Switch tabs to check something unrelated to work.
- Copy something, forget to paste it, and copy it again.
- Save a file in the wrong place and search for it.
- Close the wrong tab and reopen it.

### Transitions

- Finish one task and pause before choosing the next.
- Think about what to do next.
- Start walking, then turn back to retrieve an item.
- Realize keys, wallet, phone, or badge are missing.
- Search for a misplaced object.
- Put something down and forget where.
- Move an object to make room for another.
- Open a door, step through, and turn back to close it.
- Hold a door for someone.
- Wait for an elevator that takes longer than expected.
- Press the elevator button again.
- Let someone exit before entering.
- Wait for a computer or application to become responsive.
- Sit down, realize the chair or desk needs adjusting, and stand again.
- Start a task, remember another task, and switch to it.
- Finish an activity early and fill the remaining time with the phone.
- Run late and rush several steps.
- Finish an activity late and shorten the next one.

- Adjust the chair height or lumbar support.
- Move the monitor or keyboard.
- Plug in a laptop or charger.
- Connect headphones and troubleshoot the connection.
- Mute and unmute during a call.
- Check whether the microphone is working.
- Wait for another person to join a meeting.
- Ask someone to repeat themselves.
- Read a message while listening to a meeting.
- Take notes, then stop taking notes.
- Look at the clock during a meeting.
- Finish a task and spend a minute deciding what to do next.
- Re-read a document because concentration was lost.
- Make a typo, notice it, and correct it.
- Run a check, wait for the result, and inspect the output.
- Encounter an error and try the same operation again.
- Search for a file or an earlier conversation.
- Save work more than once.
- Refill a water bottle and forget to bring it back.
- Go to the break room and encounter a coworker.
- Exchange a greeting or a short bit of small talk.
- Wait for the microwave while looking at a phone.
- Find the break room occupied and return later.
- Notice a coworker's conversation and listen for a moment.

### Cognitive and emotional

These change the next action even when the body barely moves.

- Remember an unfinished task.
- Worry about a deadline.
- Mentally rehearse an upcoming conversation.
- Replay an awkward interaction.
- Remember something embarrassing.
- Daydream briefly.
- Lose track of time.
- Become distracted by a noise or a movement.
- Feel hungry and decide not to eat yet.
- Feel thirsty and postpone water.
- Feel tired and consider a nap.
- Become bored and look for stimulation.
- Feel motivated and start a task early.
- Feel frustrated and avoid a task for a while.
- Forget what they meant to do.
- Make a small decision, reconsider it, and reverse it.
- Notice that the room is too hot or too cold.
- Become sharper after coffee, or dip in the afternoon.
- Think of something to buy, search for it, and abandon the search.
- Remember a personal obligation while working.

### Evening and bedtime

- Change into comfortable clothes right after getting home.
- Check the fridge before deciding what to eat.
- Snack before dinner.
- Sit down, then stand because something was forgotten.
- Pause the television to answer a message.
- Rewind a scene because attention wandered.
- Scroll streaming options for several minutes.
- Start a show and decide it is not the one.
- Check the time and count the hours of sleep left.
- Feel too tired to clean up after dinner.
- Return to the kitchen for a drink.
- Check the front door lock.
- Check the alarm twice.
- Search for the phone, then find it already in bed.
- Adjust the pillow more than once.
- Turn the television down because it is loud.
- Get settled, feel too hot, and uncover.
- Feel too cold and pull the blanket up.
- Roll from one side to the other.
- Try to sleep, notice the mind is still going, and check the time.
- Wake briefly and change position.
- Hear a noise and listen.
- An itch, or a shift of the blanket.
- Stay in bed when the alarm starts.
